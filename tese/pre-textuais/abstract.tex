\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
\begin{otherlanguage*}{english}
Diagnosing overfitting in deep neural networks currently relies on a labelled
validation set that is representative and sufficiently large --- a condition
often unattainable in medical imaging, where annotation is costly and requires
expert knowledge. This dissertation investigates whether statistical complexity
measures computed exclusively from network weights, without access to any
label, can serve as markers of the onset of overfitting. Four measures are
considered and tracked across training epochs: the
Lopez-Ruiz--Mancini--Calbet (LMC) complexity, defined as the product of Shannon
entropy and the statistical distance from equilibrium; sample entropy
(SampEn); its two-dimensional variant (SampEn2D); and the multiscale version
(MSE). Two complementary experimental designs are employed. In the first,
overfitting is induced by subsampling the training set across three subsets of
the MedMNIST repository, with the measures applied to convolutional and batch
normalisation parameters; in this regime, LMC exhibits a peak preceding the
minimum validation loss, with a lead time on the order of twelve to seventeen
epochs. In the second, memorisation is induced by controlled label corruption,
preserving data volume, and the measures are computed over the entire dense
layer. In this regime, with eight runs and strictly paired controls, SampEn2D
inflects $7.5 \pm 0.9$ epochs before validation accuracy degrades, with an
amplitude ratio of $5.7$ between runs with and without memorisation
($p = 0.00016$), a result replicated on the DenseNet-121 architecture
($4.3$ times; $p = 0.0016$). LMC complexity, however, fails to discriminate
memorisation in the dense layer ($1.1$ times; $p = 0.19$). The findings
indicate that the ability to mark overfitting depends jointly on the measure
employed, on the group of weights observed, and on the mechanism that gives
rise to the loss of generalisation.

\noindent
\textbf{Keywords}: Statistical complexity; Sample entropy; Overfitting; Deep
learning; Medical imaging; Memorisation; LMC complexity.
\end{otherlanguage*}
\end{resumo}
