\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
\begin{otherlanguage*}{english}
Since its recent resurgence, deep learning (DL) has been widely applied to
medical imaging tasks, achieving remarkable success across various applications
and ushering in what is known as the era of artificial intelligence (AI). The
success of AI is largely driven by the availability of annotated large-scale
datasets (big data) and advances in high-performance computing. However,
medical imaging presents unique challenges to DL approaches, which still lack
full understanding of their structural and dynamic evolutions. This project
aims to validate a method for analyzing network architectures and studying
structural and dynamic changes in different medical imaging problems.
Applications such as segmentation and diagnostic support based on DL imaging
will be explored. To estimate the statistical complexity of network weights,
the Lopez-Ruiz--Mancini--Calbet (LMC) complexity will be
applied---defined as the product of Shannon entropy and statistical distance
from equilibrium---tested as a metric of self-organization. The project also
explores SampEn, SampEn2D, and MSE as tools to estimate structural
irregularities. This study seeks to understand internal processes in DL
networks better when applied to medical imaging and identify the best
architectures for specific applications.

\vspace{\onelineskip}
\noindent
\textbf{Keywords}: Medical images; Artificial intelligence; Deep learning;
Structural irregularity; Statistical complexity; Diagnostic support; Image
segmentation; Image-guided therapy.
\end{otherlanguage*}
\end{resumo}
