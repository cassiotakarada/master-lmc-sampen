\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
\begin{otherlanguage*}{english}
Diagnosing overfitting in deep neural networks currently relies on a labelled
validation set that is representative and sufficiently large --- a condition
often unattainable in medical imaging, where annotation is costly and requires
expert knowledge. This dissertation investigates whether statistical complexity
measures computed exclusively from network weights, without access to any
label, can serve as markers of the onset of overfitting. Three measures are
considered: the Lopez-Ruiz--Mancini--Calbet (LMC) complexity, defined as the
product of Shannon entropy and the statistical distance from equilibrium;
sample entropy (SampEn); and its two-dimensional variant (SampEn2D), computed
at every epoch over the dense-layer weights of networks trained on the MedNIST
dataset. Memorisation is induced by controlled label corruption, preserving
data volume. With eight runs and strictly paired controls, SampEn2D
inflects $7.5 \pm 0.9$ epochs before validation accuracy degrades, with an
amplitude ratio of $5.7$ between runs with and without memorisation
($p = 0.00016$), a result replicated on the DenseNet-121 architecture
($4.3$ times; $p = 0.0016$). LMC complexity, however, fails to discriminate
memorisation in the dense layer ($1.1$ times; $p = 0.19$). The findings
indicate that the ability to mark overfitting depends on the measure employed.

\vspace{\onelineskip}
\noindent
\textbf{Keywords}: Statistical complexity; Sample entropy; Overfitting; Deep
learning; Medical imaging; Memorisation; LMC complexity.
\end{otherlanguage*}
\end{resumo}
